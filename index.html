The Global Semiconductor Supply Chain

From Integrated Supply Chains to a Multi-Track Tech World

Semiconductors sit at the center of modern life. Every phone call, online purchase, GPS route, medical device, or electric vehicle depends on them. Yet the path from raw materials to a finished chip is one of the most intricate global supply chains ever created. Dozens of countries contribute, thousands of steps unfold in cleanrooms, and a single defect can ruin an entire batch.

This scroll-through page introduces you to the technical backbone of chipmaking—from design, to fabrication, to assembly and testing. As you move through each segment, you'll see how different regions of the world specialize in different parts of this chain, and why no single country can make an advanced chip entirely on its own.

First stage: Design

Where ideas become architectures.

Before a chip ever touches silicon, everything begins with design.

Design

The design of chips are precise instructions, known as masks or layouts, that dictate where each transistor will sit on the silicon wafer.

Two important inputs at the design stage are:

high-end Electronic Design Automation (EDA) tools

third-party IP cores

Companies define the chip’s purpose, such as mobile processor, AI accelerator, automotive controller, etc. Then, using high-end EDA tools, engineers create architectural blueprints including logic blocks, memory layout, power management, and interconnects. Third-party IP cores (e.g., patented ARM CPU designs, GPU blocks) are often integrated to speed development.

Fabless vs IDM

Not all companies design chips in the same way. The semiconductor industry is split into two major models — fabless and IDM (Integrated Device Manufacturer) — each shaping global supply chains differently.

Many of today’s most recognizable semiconductor giants do not manufacture their own chips. They follow a fabless model: they focus exclusively on design and rely on specialized manufacturers to build their chips. They also often spend an extensive amount of revenue on R&D.

On the other side are IDMs, companies that handle the entire process from design and fabrication to assembly and testing. These firms control their own factories and supply chains, giving them greater independence but much higher costs.

The United States leads in the semiconductor design segment, with U.S. firms capturing more than 40 percent of global IC design market share, which includes EDA, semiconductor IP, and design services revenue.

Some of the key American fabless companies include AMD, Broadcom, NVIDIA, Qualcomm, Marvell Technology. Most of them are located in California.
